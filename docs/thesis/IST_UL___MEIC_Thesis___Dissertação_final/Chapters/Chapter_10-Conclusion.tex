\section{Summary and Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion_summary}

This dissertation addressed a methodological rather than purely technical problem. \ac{AI} is increasingly present throughout the \ac{SDLC}, yet its benefits depend on the process surrounding its use. The evidence synthesised in \Cref{chap:intro,chap:slr} shows that functional correctness does not imply security, perceived and measured productivity can diverge, and increased output does not necessarily improve delivery outcomes. Existing software-development methodologies were designed around human contributors, while emerging AI-oriented approaches remain fragmented in their treatment of lifecycle integration, oversight and evaluation. The resulting research problem is the absence of structured and repeatable guidance for integrating \ac{AI} across the \ac{SDLC} while preserving human oversight, quality, security and accountability.

Two artefacts were produced. The first is the framework in \Cref{chap:proposal}: Strategic Alignment followed by six lifecycle phases, each defining the permitted AI contribution, required output, accountable hat and exit control (\Cref{tab:phase_mapping}). Its governance mechanisms include Bolts, the Consistency Factor, Spec-Anchored Continuity, five quality gates, three governance metrics and three explicit Maintenance loop-backs. Responsibilities are expressed as functions rather than fixed organisational roles. The second artefact is Apex (\Cref{chap:apex}), a working instantiation used to demonstrate that these constructs can be implemented and evaluated.

The evaluation deliberately separated evidence about the framework from evidence about Apex. The demonstration carried a complete product over nine calendar days, growing from six planned epics to nine delivered, fifty-four stories carried through the lifecycle to a deployed increment, while exercising all three loop-back routes (\Cref{sec:results_demonstration}). The framework was also assessed against five predefined analytical criteria, of which two were \emph{met} and three \emph{partially met}, and discussed with five practitioners and one Agile/Scrum expert. Apex itself was evaluated through an unmoderated nine-task study with thirteen participants, producing a \ac{SUS} mean of \textbf{56.73} and a Raw \ac{NASA-TLX} aggregate of \textbf{21.5/100}. A usability result for the tool is therefore not treated as evidence for or against the framework itself. Against the four dimensions of the research problem, the outcome is as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.6cm, itemsep=0.35em, topsep=0.2em]

\item \textbf{Placement. } AI-supported activity can be bounded at phase granularity by defining, for every phase, its AI contribution, output, accountable hat and exit control. The decomposition survived instantiation without any phase being merged, split or omitted and was independently recognised in expert interview. The qualification is that some lower-level construct assumptions remain insufficiently explicit.

\item \textbf{Governance. } Specification-based gates, stable identifiers, Spec-Anchored Continuity and server-side validation make AI contributions traceable and reviewable. Human-in-the-loop support is structurally realised because generation alone cannot satisfy a phase gate. Accountability remains weaker. AI-attributable defects are observable only through a proxy, and governance recommendations were observed operating on stale evidence.

\item \textbf{Realisation. } Of twelve mapped constructs, \textbf{ten are realised as specified}, \textbf{one only as a proxy}, and \textbf{one partially}. Implementation also exposed framework assumptions. Bolt Cycle Time was initially measured through the wrong proxy, while spec-drift detection revealed an unstated requirement for story-level specification ownership. Realisation does not imply reliability, however. The Testing Gate could be satisfied by tests that no longer exercised the current system, and no existing gate covers a destructive action issued inside a phase.

\item \textbf{Practitioner support. } Cognitive workload was comparatively low, but usability was not. The overall \ac{SUS} score was grade D, error legibility was the weakest \ac{UX} area, and \textbf{9 of 13} participants identified density, orientation or navigation as their principal difficulty. Apex therefore imposes relatively modest workload once understood, but insufficiently supports first contact.

\end{itemize}

The dissertation's contributions are consequently fourfold: the systematic synthesis of evidence on AI participation across the \ac{SDLC}; the proposed framework of phases, gates, responsibilities, metrics and specification anchoring; Apex as an executable instantiation exposing both successful and resistant constructs; and an empirical evaluation that separates claims about the methodology from claims about its implementation. The work does \emph{not} establish that the framework improves delivered software quality, since no instrument in this study directly measures that outcome.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.6cm, itemsep=0.35em, topsep=0.2em]

\item \textbf{Public repository.} The complete Apex implementation and its documentation, including every architectural, design and governance record referenced throughout this dissertation, are published in full at \url{https://github.com/thomastabs/apex}, with the deployed instantiation itself available at \url{https://apex-bolt.com}. \item \textbf{Extended abstract.} A self-contained technical article accompanies the dissertation, summarising the framework, its instantiation and evaluation for readers who will not consult the full document.

\item \textbf{Dissertation and defence.} This document and its academic defence communicate the complete work and expose its design decisions to examination by reviewers who did not participate in its construction or evaluation.

\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

These limitations concern the artefacts and research as a whole, rather than the evaluation-specific threats discussed in \Cref{sec:threats}.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.6cm, itemsep=0.4em]

\item \textbf{The framework is an original synthesis.} Its individual mechanisms are grounded in prior work, including \ac{AI-DLC}~\cite{AWS:2025aidlc}, Team Topologies and the Spotify model~\cite{Kniberg:2012sp, Skelton:2019tt}, and specification-driven development~\cite{Thoughtworks:2025SD, Farrag:2026sd}, but no source provides the combined blueprint proposed here. Implementation exposed several unresolved assumptions: AI Defect Escape Rate is only observable through a weaker linked-issue proxy; technical-specification drift lacks story-level ownership; destructive operations inside a phase fall outside the gate model; and advisory governance can report high confidence from stale evidence. Whether an effective in-phase control can be added without undermining the phase-gate model remains open.

\item \textbf{Some grounding evidence is recent or non-peer-reviewed.} \ac{AI-DLC}'s productivity claims are vendor-reported~\cite{AWS:2025aidlc}; specification-driven development remains an emerging practice~\cite{Thoughtworks:2025SD}; one key dependability argument is a preprint~\cite{Farrag:2026sd}; and several motivating results come from industry telemetry and surveys~\cite{DORA:2025ai, StackOverflow:2025dev, Faros:2025par, Veracode:2025gen}. The argument therefore rests on convergence across independent sources rather than on any single reported effect size.

\item \textbf{Governance-standard terminology does not imply compliance.} The framework is positioned against the functions and vocabulary of the \ac{NIST} \ac{AI} Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001:2023~\cite{NIST:2023rmf, ISO/IEC:2023ai}, but neither the framework nor Apex has been audited or certified against either standard.

\item \textbf{The evidence concerns early adoption in a narrow setting.} The end-to-end demonstration covers nine days of solo use by the artefacts' designer; the usability study covers one approximately hour-long session per participant, one seeded project and one project-management tool; and the planned independent organisational demonstration did not run. The study therefore cannot establish sustained gate discipline, long-term specification integrity, organisational adoption, metric behaviour under management incentives, or transferability to teams that did not design the framework.

\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

Future work follows directly from the limitations and empirical findings above:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.6cm, itemsep=0.4em]

\item \textbf{Evaluate the framework across organisations and team sizes.} The only complete lifecycle demonstration used one practitioner holding every hat, while the thirteen evaluation participants followed a fixed seeded task rather than applying the framework to their own work. The weakest-supported framework \ac{UX} item concerned fit to real teams. Future studies should therefore distribute hats across independent practitioners and organisations that did not design the framework.

\item \textbf{Evaluate alternative project-management backends.} Additional adapters demonstrate architectural portability but not behavioural parity (\Cref{sec:apex_architecture}). Both the demonstration and evaluation used the first-class backend. Running the framework against another supported tool would test tool-independence empirically and help separate unfamiliarity with Apex from unfamiliarity with the project-management system.

\item \textbf{Replace proxy metrics with direct measurement.} AI Defect Escape Rate currently observes only defects later linked to a deployed story; the demonstration recorded \textbf{19 Fix-Bolts across 14 stories}, while the escape proxy recorded only \textbf{one}. Direct production telemetry is therefore required, together with an explicit framework definition of who owns attribution of defects to generated logic.

\item \textbf{Extend governance to AI cost.} The framework should add an \textbf{AI Token and Compute Cost} metric at Bolt granularity, recorded directly from provider usage data. This should be paired with an accountable AI-cost governance hat, detection of unstaffed responsibilities, and risk- or phase-tiered model selection extending the existing Fast Lane/Secure Lane principle.

\item \textbf{Improve support for the Design phase.} Task~4 produced the highest mental demand (\textbf{47.7}), temporal demand (\textbf{40.8}) and aggregate workload (\textbf{30.4}), while the next-highest mental-demand task scored 26.9. Rather than adding a heavier gate, future versions should provide worked examples and structured interim critique of usability, scenario coherence and architectural fit before whole-artefact review.

\item \textbf{Treat first-contact usability as a non-negotiable requirement.} \ac{SUS} Usability averaged \textbf{59.86}, compared with Learnability at \textbf{44.23}, with Learnability ranging from \textbf{0-100} against Usability's \textbf{12.50-87.50}. Error legibility was the weakest \ac{UX} item, mental demand was the largest workload subscale, and the Portuguese subgroup averaged \textbf{44.17} compared with \textbf{60.50} for English respondents, although $n=3$ prevents broader inference. Because practitioners encounter the methodology through Apex, poor orientation or visual complexity can be experienced as methodological difficulty. Future redesign should therefore prioritise visual simplicity, first-contact orientation, navigation and error legibility as prerequisites for practical framework adoption.

\end{itemize}
