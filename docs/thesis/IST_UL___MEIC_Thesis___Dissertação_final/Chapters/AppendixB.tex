\chapter{Evaluation Instruments}
\label{chapter:appendixB}

This appendix reproduces the evaluation instruments used in \Cref{chap:evaluation}, including consent and demographics, workload, usability, the author-designed \ac{UX} questionnaire, and interview guides. Instruments were available in English and Portuguese; scoring, interpretation and results are reported in \Cref{chap:evaluation}.

\section{Raw NASA Task Load Index}
\label{sec:appB_tlx}

The Raw \ac{NASA-TLX}~\cite{Hart:1988TLX,Hart:2006TLX} was administered after tasks 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9. Responses used a 0-10 scale, rescaled to 0-100, with Performance presented last using reversed anchors; these adaptations are explained in \Cref{sec:eval_instruments_procedure}. Portuguese participants received direct translations with the same subscales, order and anchors.

\section{Interview Guides}
\label{sec:appB_interviews}

Two semi-structured interview guides evaluated the framework rather than Apex in separate, unrecorded 30-45 minute sessions. Questions were used flexibly and freely as prompts.

\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textbf{Practitioners Guide}} \\*
\midrule

1 &
Describe how your team moves from an idea to production, where AI appears in that process, who introduced it, and what most often goes wrong. \\

2 &
Which of the framework's six phase-end decisions would your team genuinely make, which might be rubber-stamped, and which phase would face the most resistance? \\

3 &
How does your team currently work without a prior specification, what would the framework change, and where might that create unnecessary delay? \\

4 &
What conditions would support adoption for one project, and what would cause abandonment after two sprints? \\

5 &
Who would need to own the framework for it to persist, and does that role exist in your team? \\

6 &
If Apex disappeared but the framework remained, which parts would you continue to use? \\

7 &
When AI-generated code fails in production, who is accountable today, and would the framework change that? \\

8 &
Does the recorded history of decisions and accountability have value outside the development team? \\

9 &
What is missing from the framework, and what important question was not asked? \\

\addlinespace[0.8em]

\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textbf{Agile and Scrum Experts Guide}} \\*
\midrule

1 &
How would you describe the framework in your own words? \\

2 &
Where does it align with or depart from Scrum and Agile, and are those departures justified by AI-specific changes? \\

3 &
Are the explicit phase gates useful guardrails or a return to Waterfall-style stage gating? \\

4 &
Does treating roles as hats work in practice, or does it collapse into one person holding every responsibility? \\

5 &
Which required artefacts would you add or remove, and is the documentation proportionate to its value? \\

6 &
What kinds of teams are poorly suited to the framework, and what would a coach need to teach for correct adoption? \\

7 &
Under what conditions would you recommend the framework to a client, and when would you refuse? \\

8 &
What is the strongest argument for the framework, and what is its weakest point? \\

\end{longtable}
