# Interview notes - Jorge Andrade (Vodafone Agile Coach)

Live framework presentation + Apex demo. Written up from contemporaneous
notes (translated from the original Portuguese), consistent with this
study's stated interview methodology (notes, not a recording/transcript).

Not one of the study's N=12 unmoderated participants and not part of the

## A. Organisational context - AI adoption maturity at Vodafone

- It's difficult to coordinate and use in-house tools/frameworks that are
  intrinsically embedded in the company. AI should function as an **aid**,
  not as something meant to cover the **entire** SWE lifecycle.
- Ideally, teams would be agile teams composed of **humans and AIs**, with
  humans managing those AIs.
- Right now, adapting frameworks to AI - or searching for one, or adapting
  an existing one - is **very unstable and chaotic**.
- AI is putting the focus on **speed**, but it's unclear whether it's
  actually making outcomes **better**.

## B. Governance gap - token/AI usage management

- Feedback from Jorge's own developers: AI doesn't solve everything; there
  are always people who are less AI-friendly; some things have been
  resolved well; open topics remain around **management** and **token
  consumption**.
- Token usage is managed **centrally**; the AI tool in question is the
  **only** one developers are permitted to use.
- There is **no role/profile** responsible for managing tokens, and **no AI
  specialist** - a real gap, to the point that they're currently trying to
  hire a **Head of AI**.
- **Suggested as thesis Future Work:** token/cost management - is AI a cost
  or a benefit? What is gained? What is lost?
- Real gap, stated directly: AI is **not** fully embedded across the SDLC -
  it's complex.

## C. Reaction to the framework's design

- A framework like this - with the roles and the rest - instantiated by a
  tool that actually applies it, **makes the framework tangible**.
- Typical company frameworks impose a heavy structure with closed/rigid
  roles. He liked that this framework **doesn't lock roles down**, and
  specifically liked the **"hats"** framing.
- He liked the initial **Mob Elaboration** ritual - gathering requirements
  more effectively with the client. Putting ideation into a single shared
  ritual helps get everyone on the same page; this stood out to him as
  different from what he's used to.
- Found the **metrics** interesting - bring a different freshness to a
  delivery.
- Positive remark on the process's overall lightness (source wording "Boa
  ligeiramento" is ambiguous in the original - likely "boa ligeireza,"
  good lightness/agility).

## D. Apex UI/tool feedback (live demo)

- Needs **tooltips or framing/context** - the tool still needs to be
  "productised" for easy comprehension.
- Suggested a **tutorial**.
- In the overall/global context, navigation became confusing - his own
  reaction mid-demo: *"where does this go next???"*
